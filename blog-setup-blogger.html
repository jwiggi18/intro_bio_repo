Set Up Your Course Blog (Blogger)

Complete by Sunday of Week 1  ·  Submit your blog URL in Canvas

Use this page only if you are setting up your blog on Blogger instead of WordPress. The assignment requirements, points, and due date are identical either way. Use whichever platform gives you less trouble — you do not need to use both. (Looking for the WordPress instructions instead? Go here.)

⚠ Your blog is a public webpage.

Anyone on the internet can read it, including:

  • Future employers
  • Graduate and professional programs
  • People you do and don't know

Note that search engines index these pages (they permanently store and make your posts searchable, even after you delete them). Write so that your future self will be glad this is who you presented to the world.

Before you begin, decide how you want to present yourself.

Step 1: Create Your Blog

Create a free Blogger blog

  1. Go to blogger.com and sign in with a Google account. If you already have a Gmail address (including a school Google account), you can use that; otherwise create a free Google account first.
  2. Click Create your blog (or the + New Blog button if you land on a dashboard).
  3. Choose a title for your blog. This is the display name visitors see, not your web address. These blogs are public: do not use your name.
  4. Choose a blog address (URL). Whatever available name you type becomes your free address automatically, in the form yourblogname.blogspot.com. Choose something appropriate for a public school-associated blog.
    Examples: biol1113-fall2026.blogspot.com or okstate-scienceblog.blogspot.com or curiousaboutbiology.blogspot.com
  5. Pick a display theme (any of the free default themes is fine) and click through to reach your dashboard. Blogger does not have paid plans or a checkout step — everything on Blogger is free, so there is no upgrade wall to run into.

Step 2: Make Sure Your Blog Is Public

Confirm your visibility settings

  1. In your Blogger dashboard, go to Settings → Privacy.
  2. Check that "Visible to search engines" is turned on and the blog is not set to private.
  3. Visit your blog URL in a new browser window (or an incognito window) to confirm a visitor can see it without logging in.

Step 3: Write Your First Post

Something that brings me joy 10 pts

Your first post is low-stakes on purpose. Write about something that genuinely brings you joy: a place, a person, a food, a hobby, an animal, a song, a memory. There is no wrong answer. Use good internet safety etiquette: do not share anything that could compromise your safety, including specific locations, last names, or other identifying details.

Requirements:

  • At least one paragraph (5+ sentences).
  • At least one image of your own creation — a photo you took, a drawing, or something you made digitally.
  • Published publicly before the deadline, not saved as a draft.

To publish: In your Blogger dashboard, click New Post (the pencil icon). Write your post, then click Publish in the top right. Make sure you click Publish, not just Save.

Step 4: Submit Your Blog URL

Submit the right URL

Submit your blog homepage URL, not the URL of your first post.

Submit this: your blog homepage
https://yourblogname.blogspot.com
Not this: a post URL
https://yourblogname.blogspot.com/2026/08/something-that-brings-me-joy.html

Your classmates will use your homepage URL to find your blog all semester.

How to find your homepage URL: Click View Blog in your Blogger dashboard. Copy the URL from your browser's address bar.

General Blog Guidelines

What your blog is for

Starting in Week 2, you'll write a blog post each week connecting that week's biological concepts to something in your own life: a news story, a personal experience, a question you have, something you observed. Your blog is a place to think out loud in public, practice science communication, and make connections that go beyond the textbook.

Your posts do not need to be perfect. They need to be:

  • Honest and thoughtful
  • Written at the level of a college freshman just learning this content, not a PhD biologist or a professional writer

Write for an audience that is curious but not a biologist:

  • Explain terms
  • Don't assume background knowledge

Cite any sources you use. A simple hyperlink is fine for informal posts.

All work must be your own.
Do not use AI to write your posts.
Your voice is the point.

Internet safety etiquette

Your blog is a public webpage, visible to anyone. Every post you write should follow these guidelines:

  • Do not share your last name, school name, home city, or any other detail that identifies your physical location or full identity.
  • Do not share photos that reveal your location or the faces of people who haven't consented.
  • Do not share personal information about family members, friends, or classmates.
  • When in doubt, leave it out. If you're not sure whether something is safe to post, it isn't.

This is not about paranoia. It is about building good habits for writing on the public internet, which you will do for the rest of your life.

Respectful writing and commenting

Your blog and your comments are extensions of a shared classroom. Everything you write should reflect how you'd want to be treated.

  • Write about ideas, not people. Disagree with a claim or interpretation, not with a person.
  • If a classmate's post has an error, you can note it kindly and constructively, or let it go. There is no version of "actually, you're wrong" that belongs in a blog comment.
  • Be especially careful with topics that touch on health, identity, culture, or belief. These come up in biology. Approach them with curiosity, not judgment.
  • If you see something in a classmate's blog or comment that concerns you, contact me directly rather than responding publicly.

Commenting on classmates' blogs

Each week starting in Week 2, you'll comment on two classmates' blogs. The Class Blog Directory has a randomizer that assigns you two blogs each week. Use it to find your assigned blogs, then leave a substantive comment on a post from that week.

A substantive comment does at least one of these things:

  • Asks a genuine question about something in the post.
  • Makes a connection to your own experience or to course content.
  • Respectfully adds information or a different perspective.

"Great post!" does not count. Neither does a single sentence. Aim for 3 to 5 sentences minimum.

You'll confirm your comments in Canvas via a short declaration quiz each week.
