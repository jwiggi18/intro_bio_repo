# The Membrane Arc: Zoom-Out Map

**Read this when you're lost in the details.**

Weeks 2–4 taught as one integrated arc. Chemistry of life is not split off into
separate intro lectures. Every chemistry concept is introduced at the moment the
membrane story needs it.

Anchor case: **cholera.**
All OpenStax links verified July 31, 2026.
Week numbers match `Weekly_topic_schedule_DRAFT.xlsx` (topic_schedule tab), as of July 31, 2026.

---

## The one-sentence version

> A membrane forms because water rejects oil, which creates an inside and an outside,
> which creates the problem of getting things across, which cells solve with proteins
> and gradients, and cholera is what happens when one of those gradients is released
> all at once.

If a piece of content doesn't serve that sentence, it doesn't go in the arc.

---

## The test for what to teach

> **Does this concept get used again later in the course?**
>
> If understanding something later requires it, teach it.
> If it only explains itself, cut it.

## Video 2: The Tour

**Organizing principle: a boundary has three jobs.** Matter, information, identity.
Every component belongs to exactly one. Not a parts list, a job list.

**Primary topics**

- Recap: hydrophobic core rejects charge, passes small nonpolar molecules
- Explicit "this is a tour, not a catalog" framing
- **Matter:** straight through, then channels, then carriers, then pumps
- Glucose as the case that forces carriers to exist (uncharged but big and polar)
- **Information:** same rule, two routes. Steroids cross and use internal receptors.
  Charged/large/polar signals cannot, so the receptor spans the membrane
- **Identity:** glycoproteins, glycolipids, the sugar coat as name tag and doorknob

**Cut from this video**

- **Cholesterol.** Only does fluidity, breaks the three-job structure. Put it wherever
  fluid mosaic is covered
- "The messenger never enters" as a general claim about signaling. **Wrong**, and it
  contradicts OpenStax 9.1, which explicitly teaches internal receptors and names
  steroid hormones. It is only true of membrane-spanning receptors

**Links forward to**

|---|---|
| Channels | Video 3 aquaporins, Video 5 CFTR, Wk 5 action potentials |
| Carriers | Video 5 (oral rehydration cotransporter), Wk 13 |
| Pumps | Video 4, Wk 5 Na⁺/K⁺, Wk 13 ATP synthase |
| Internal receptors / steroids | Wk 5 endocrine, Wks 7-9 gene regulation |
| Membrane receptors | Video 5 cholera toxin, Wk 4 signaling |
| Glycocalyx | Video 5 (the toxin's dock), Wks 5-6 immune self vs non-self |

## Video 4: Who's Paying

**The conceptual hinge. Shortest video, most load-bearing.**

**Primary topics**

- Channels vs. pumps
- Pumps spend ATP and have direction; channels are free and follow the gradient
- Gradients are built, not found
- A gradient is stored energy
- Equilibrium is death

**Links forward to**

| Concept | Reappears in |
|---|---|
| Gradient as stored battery | **Wk 5 action potentials, Wk 13 chemiosmosis, Wk 14 photosynthesis** |
| Pumps cost ATP | Wks 5-6 physiology, Wk 13 metabolism |
| Channels release, pumps build | Video 5 (CFTR is a release valve) |

This video is why **Week 5 action potentials** and **Week 13 chemiosmosis** will make sense.
Do not merge it into another video.

**Readings**

- [5.3 Active Transport](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/5-3-active-transport)

## Video 5: Cholera, Explained

**Primary topics**

- *Vibrio cholerae* colonizes the small intestine and never enters a cell
- Cholera toxin docks on a surface glycolipid (callback to the glycocalyx)
- The toxin jams a signaling switch permanently on
- The signal opens CFTR, a chloride channel
- Cl⁻ leaves down a gradient the cell paid to build
- Na⁺ follows the charge
- Water follows through aquaporins, working perfectly, on a false signal
- Oral rehydration reverses the gradient using a transporter the toxin can't reach

**Links backward to**, every previous video in the arc. That's the point.

**Links forward to**

| Concept | Reappears in |
|---|---|
| CFTR | **Wks 7-9 Genetics, cystic fibrosis, the same protein failing the opposite way** |
| G protein signaling | Wk 4 signaling, **Wk 5 endocrine system** |
| Receptor binding | **Wks 5-6 immune recognition and disease** |
| Carrier frequency / heterozygote advantage | Wk 11 population genetics |
