Cell Communication

Module opens Monday  ·  All work due Sunday, September 6, by 11:59 PM

Objectives

By the end of this week, you will be able to:

  • Distinguish direct-contact cell signaling (juxtacrine signaling and gap junctions) from signaling across a gap using a ligand (autocrine, paracrine, and endocrine signaling).
  • Distinguish autocrine, paracrine, and endocrine signaling from each other, identify the major features of each, and an example from our learning materials of where each is used.
  • Identify the two major classes of receptors and what passes through each (something physical or a signal).
  • Describe the three stages of cell signaling (reception, transduction, and cellular response), and explain how phosphorylation and second messengers amplify a signal inside the cell.
  • Compare enzyme-linked receptors and G-protein coupled receptors in terms of how ligand binding activates each one and how signal transduction propagates.
  • Explain how cholera toxin disrupts the normal G-protein signaling cycle (GTP/GDP hydrolysis) to keep the chloride channel open, causing severe fluid loss.
  • Apply the concepts of cell signaling and macromolecule synthesis/breakdown to trace how a single bacterial toxin can hijack a normal communication pathway.
  • Explain how dehydration synthesis joins monomers into polymers, and how hydrolysis reverses that process, using amino acids and monosaccharides as examples, and apply this general principle to GTP and ATP hydrolysis.
